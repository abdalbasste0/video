# THE D.B. COOPER MYSTERY: series bible

A true, unsolved case told as a 4-part animated mystery. Narration in English, documentary tone,
past tense, short sentences. Only well-documented facts are stated as facts; anything uncertain is
framed as "investigators believe", "some say", "no one knows".

## Facts we rely on
- Wed 24 Nov 1971, the day before Thanksgiving. Northwest Orient Flight 305, a Boeing 727,
  Portland (PDX) to Seattle (SEA), about a 30-minute flight.
- A quiet man in a dark suit, white shirt, black clip-on tie and raincoat, mid-40s, bought a
  one-way ticket under the name "Dan Cooper". Sat near the back, ordered a bourbon and soda.
- After takeoff he handed a flight attendant a note. She assumed it was a phone number and put it
  away; he told her to read it: he had a bomb. In his briefcase: wires and red sticks.
- Demands: $200,000 in $20 bills, four parachutes, and a fuel truck waiting in Seattle.
- The jet circled Puget Sound for about two hours while the money (10,000 twenty-dollar bills,
  serial numbers recorded by the FBI) and the parachutes were gathered.
- In Seattle the 36 passengers were released in exchange for the money and parachutes.
  He kept the pilots, the flight engineer and one flight attendant.
- He ordered a flight toward Mexico City via Reno: low and slow, under 10,000 feet,
  landing gear down, cabin unpressurized, rear airstair lowered.
- Around 8 p.m. a warning light showed the rear stairs had opened. Around 8:13 p.m. the crew felt
  a sudden bump in the tail. That is believed to be the moment he jumped: at night, in rain,
  freezing cold, over the forests of southwest Washington, in a suit and loafers.
- The jet landed in Reno. The cabin was empty.
- He left behind his black clip-on tie with a tie clip, and some cigarette butts.
- The press reported the name as "D.B. Cooper" after a mix-up; the name stuck.
- One of the largest manhunts in U.S. history found nothing.
- Feb 1980: an 8-year-old boy digging on a Columbia River sandbank (Tena Bar) found about
  $5,800 in rotting $20 bills. The serial numbers matched the ransom.
- Copycat hijackings followed in 1972, which led to airport passenger screening, and to the
  "Cooper vane", a device that stops a 727's rear stairs from opening in flight.
- Many suspects over the years; none proven. The FBI ended its active investigation in July 2016.

## Episodes
1. **The Man in Seat 18C**: the passenger, the note, the bomb, the demands, circling Seattle.
   Cliffhanger: the money is ready, and he asks for something no one expected: the rear stairs.
2. **The Jump**: Seattle exchange, passengers freed, the strange flight plan, the stairs, the
   8:13 bump, Reno: empty cabin. Cliffhanger: a man jumped into a storm in loafers. Did he live?
3. **The Money in the Sand**: the manhunt, the name mix-up, nothing found for nine years, then
   the boy and the $5,800. Cliffhanger: the only other clue is a tie.
4. **Who Was D.B. Cooper?**: the tie, the suspects, the copycats, the Cooper vane, the FBI closing
   the case in 2016. Final question to the audience.
